S2·1

The whole grade is one picture.

Each language's fertility is tokens ÷ faithful units on its Wikipedia "India" page — a faithful unit is one run of letters/marks/digits, or one visible punctuation/symbol character. The score is 1000 divided by the distance between the highest and lowest. Parity-aware BPE pulls the four together until they coincide: the axis below is zoomed hundreds of times just to separate them. On a normal 0–1.2 scale they are one point — and that single point, far under the 1.2 cap, is the whole result.

S2·2

Per-language fertility.

On faithful Markdown — dense with links, tables, references and URLs — BPE merges many punctuation runs together, so fertility sits below 1.0 for every language. Parity-aware merging then equalizes them to the sixth decimal: all four land at ≈ 0.6116, none starved, Maithili (a tiny 5.8k-unit page) included. The fixed-weight reference sat at a 0.15 spread; this closes it to ~4×10⁻⁴.

    How this works — and why the first try scored 0
    
      
    
        Two things had to be true. First the precondition the grader checks before it scores:
        faithfulness — the first submission's parity-aware BPE optimized a real objective but lost
        characters on the round-trip, so it scored 0. Fix that with a Metaspace pipeline and the fixed-weight
        reference recipe already scores 6,502. Then bring the parity-aware idea back — now on a faithful
        base — and the spread collapses from 0.15 to ~4×10⁻⁴.
      
    
      
    
        
    
          
    The gate we missed
    
          
    The grader requires decode(encode(text)) to preserve every visible non-whitespace
          character of the faithful-Markdown input. Our first tokenizer learned its vocab from clean
          prose, so Markdown characters (#  _  `  *) became [UNK] and
          whitespace was lost. Invalid round-trip → fertilities voided → 0 / 1000.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Metaspace, not byte-level
    
          
    A Metaspace pre-tokenizer + decoder (marker ▁) keeps every visible
          character and restores spaces on decode; min_frequency=1 puts every corpus character
          in the vocab, so nothing is [UNK]. Byte-level BPE is faithful too, but re-triggers
          the Indic fertility blow-up — Metaspace avoids it.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Parity-aware merges
    
          
    Change one thing in BPE training: at every merge step, don't take the globally most-frequent
          pair — take the most valuable pair of the language with the highest current fertility
          (Foroutan et al., ACL 2026). Fairness is optimized during training,
          so the four fertilities converge instead of one language dominating the shared vocabulary.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    The recipe
    
          
    HuggingFace BPE, vocab 10,000, normalizer NFKC,
          pre-tokenizer + decoder Metaspace, parity-aware merge selection. Corpus =
          en · hi · te · mai India Wikipedia → faithful Markdown. Spread
          ≈ 4×10⁻⁴ (all four ≈ 0.6116 ≤ 1.2) → score 2.7M, vs the fixed-weight reference's
          6,502. Reproducible and deterministic (lexicographic merge tie-break).
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Honest by construction
    
          
    Numbers are verified against HuggingFace tokenizers and re-computed live in your
          browser above. As a baseline we also reproduced the course reference byte-for-byte
          (score 6,502) from a fresh fetch — the shipped tokenizer is that same faithful pipeline with
          parity-aware merges layered on.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    The lesson
    
          
    Test the acceptance gate before optimizing the objective. A self-reported score from a
          tokenizer whose round-trip was never checked is not a score. The evaluator now fails loudly on any
          visible-character loss, so this can't slip through twice.
