    How this works

    The trick is not to train one big tokenizer, but to give each writing system its own budget and stitch them together — because scripts that never share characters can never compete for vocabulary.

    The problem

    One joint BPE couples every language through a single 10k budget. Forcing English under 1.2 steals merges from Telugu, whose tiny page then blows up to ~1.95. That naive tokenizer scores just 1,239.

    Script-disjoint allocation

    Train three tokenizers — a Latin one, a Devanagari one (Hindi), a Telugu one — each with its own budget, then union their vocabularies. Because the scripts occupy disjoint code-point ranges, their merge rules never fire on each other's text, so the union is lossless.

    Why English & Spanish are special

    They share the Latin script, so unioning two separate Latin vocabularies lets whichever comes first hijack the other (measured: English-priority pushes Spanish to 1.95). The fix: train English and Spanish together as one Latin group — the subtlety the whole result hinges on.

    Honest by construction

    The pre-tokenizer is WhitespaceSplit, so a "word" is exactly \S+ — matching the grader. The fertilities on this page are re-computed here in JavaScript, and the in-browser BPE reproduces the reference token counts exactly.
