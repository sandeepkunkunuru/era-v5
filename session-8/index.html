Session 8 · Modern Attention Variants

How attention works now,
in the order it was invented

Thirty mechanisms, 2014 to 2026, arranged by launch date rather than by family. Standard scaled dot-product attention comes first, because nothing after it makes sense otherwise. Everything else is somebody looking at a bill attention sends and trying to pay less of it — and every one of them charges you somewhere else. Every date here was checked against the paper, not recalled.

“Vanilla attention was not wrong, it was expensive, and every single thing that follows is somebody looking at that bill and trying to pay less of it. When you lay them out on a timeline you can watch the field change its mind.”

01The mechanism everything else modifies

Six tokens, four dimensions, every number computed live. Step through it once. Every mechanism further down this page changes exactly one of these five steps — which is the only reason the timeline is legible at all.

Two facts to carry forward.

1. Every token is compared with every other token. Six tokens give 36 scores; 10,000 give 100,000,000 — per head, per layer. That is the compute bill.

2. Attention is order-blind. Compare step 3 with step 4 above: before the mask, every query is happily scoring tokens that have not happened yet. Even with the mask, nothing in QKᵀ knows whether a token is two positions back or two thousand. Position has to be injected from outside, and seven entries in the timeline below are attempts to do it well.

02The two bills — and why they never merged

Attention charges twice, and the two charges grow at different rates. Almost every mechanism below pays down one of them, and a good number make the other worse. Reading the timeline without this distinction is what makes it look like a pile of unrelated tricks.

Attention compute — grows as T² KV-cache memory — grows as T
both indexed to T = 1,024 → 1×

The cache bill is per-conversation, and that is the whole commercial problem. Model weights load once and every user shares them. A KV cache belongs to one active sequence — so concurrency multiplies it directly. At 48 layers, 8 KV heads, head dim 128 in bf16, one user at 32,768 tokens costs 6.44 GB; eight users at 1M tokens cost 1.6 TB. That is why the interesting hardware number is memory capacity, not FLOPs.

04What the timeline shows that a list cannot

A list tells you a mechanism exists. Dates tell you what people were stuck on, how long they stayed stuck, and what actually unstuck them — which is almost never the thing you would guess. Eight things fall out of the ordering that no taxonomy contains.

Nobody optimised attention for two years.

The transformer is June 2017. The first paper attacking its quadratic cost is April 2019. In between: nothing — because at the lengths people used, T² did not yet hurt. Optimisation follows demand, not invention. The corollary is uncomfortable: a mechanism's absence from the record is evidence about what was painful, not about what was possible.

2017-06-12 → 2019-04-23 · 22 months

The two bills separated within seven months and never merged again.

Sparse Transformer (April 2019) attacks compute. MQA (November 2019) attacks the KV cache. Different authors, different institutions, different failure being felt — and from that moment the field runs two parallel threads that only meet in 2025's hybrid schedules. Grouping by family hides this completely; grouping by date makes it the first thing you see.

2019-04-23 compute · 2019-11-06 memory

The best ideas arrive years before they are usable, and what unlocks them is a kernel.

The delta rule is February 2021 and gets deployed in 2025. What changed in between was not the idea — Yang et al. (June 2024) added no new modelling contribution. They reparameterised the update so it runs as chunked matmuls instead of a sequential scan. Same maths, different memory-access pattern, four years of difference. FlashAttention (2022) is the same story told the other way: it changed nothing about what attention computes and everything about what it costs.

2021-02-22 delta rule → 2024-06-10 parallel form → 2024-12-09 gated → shipped 2025

The field changed its mind about exactness — twice.

2019–2021 says approximate: sparse patterns, then delete the softmax outright. Then FlashAttention (May 2022) shows that exact attention was never actually expensive for the reason everyone assumed — the cost was memory traffic, not arithmetic — and the approximation literature goes quiet for about two years. It returns in 2024–2025, but changed: sparsity is now trained in (NSA) rather than bolted on, and linear layers appear only alongside exact ones. The second wave learned the lesson of the first.

approximate → exact-and-cheap → approximate-but-trained

Position ends by being deleted.

Read the position lane top to bottom and each step stores less than the last. Learned table (2017): store a vector per position, and hit a wall. Sinusoidal and RoPE (2017, 2021): compute it instead — the wall becomes soft. NTK-aware and YaRN (2023): rescale the computation to stretch further. DroPE (2025): remove it entirely and recalibrate, on the argument that positional encoding helps convergence and then blocks generalisation. Eight years to get from "store position" to "position was scaffolding."

store → compute → rescale → remove

The most-adopted context-extension trick of 2023 was a Reddit post.

NTK-aware scaled RoPE (June 2023) was posted to r/LocalLLaMA by a pseudonymous author, ran on hardware people already owned, and shipped in production models before YaRN formalised parts of it two months later. If you build the timeline from arXiv alone you get the wrong story about how context extension actually happened.

2023-06-28 reddit → 2023-08-31 arXiv:2309.00071

Compromises beat extremes, and the compromise arrives late.

MQA (2019) cut the cache 8× and almost nobody shipped it, because one shared KV head cost too much quality. The interpolation — GQA — is May 2023. Four years for "use two instead of one or eight," and it was adopted near-universally within a year of arriving. What GQA added was not the idea but the uptraining recipe: you could convert a checkpoint you already had. Adoption is an engineering property, not a quality one.

2019-11-06 MQA → 2023-05-22 GQA

The answer stopped being a mechanism and became a schedule.

By late 2025 nobody picks one. Qwen3-Next runs 3 Gated DeltaNet layers to 1 attention layer; LightningLM V4's DDDGDDDG motif is 6 DeltaNet to 2 GSA — the same 3:1 ratio, reached independently. Cheap recurrent layers carry the bulk; periodic exact layers restore precise lookup. That two labs converged on one number is the strongest evidence in this whole timeline that the ratio means something, and it is only visible if you put them side by side by date.

2025-01-14 MiniMax 7:1 · 2025-09-11 Qwen3-Next 3:1 · LightningLM V4 3:1

And one thing the timeline lets you do that a list does not: guess what comes next. The pattern is that each wave takes something previously bolted on at inference time and makes it part of training — sparsity did this in 2025 (NSA), position did it in 2025 (DroPE), and the cache did it in 2024 (MLA compresses by construction rather than by eviction). The remaining bolted-on component is the schedule: every hybrid ratio in existence, including both 3:1 results above, was chosen by hand. Making the depth schedule learned rather than hand-picked is the obvious next entry, and nothing on this timeline has done it yet.

05The mechanism the syllabus missed

The assignment asks for one that was not covered, with its date and primary source.

FlashAttention — Dao, Fu, Ermon, Rudra & Ré · 27 May 2022 · arXiv:2205.14135 (v1 submission date read from the arXiv abstract page).

It is the strongest answer available, and not because it was left out by accident. Every other mechanism on the required list buys cheapness with approximation: skip some keys, share some heads, compress the cache, delete the softmax. FlashAttention buys it with nothing. The output is bit-for-bit identical to standard attention. It is 2–4× faster and uses O(T) memory instead of O(T²), and there is no quality column to report because there is no quality trade.

That matters for the timeline specifically, because it identifies the real bottleneck the preceding three years had misdiagnosed. Sparse Transformer, Longformer and linear attention all assume the enemy is arithmetic. It was not. It was writing the T×T score matrix out to HBM and reading it back — an I/O problem wearing a complexity-theory costume. Tile the computation, keep the tiles in SRAM, fuse the softmax, never materialise the matrix, recompute in the backward pass.

Leave it out and the timeline tells a clean, wrong story: attention was expensive, so the field approximated it, and got better at approximating. Put it in and the story is the true one — the field approximated attention for three years on a mispriced assumption, someone repriced it, the approximation work paused for two years, and what came back afterwards was a different and more careful kind of approximation. An entry that changes the shape of the argument is worth more than one that extends the list.

Five further uncovered mechanisms are in the timeline above, each with a verified primary source: Transformer-XL (2019-01-09, arXiv:1901.02860) — the direct ancestor of every cross-chunk memory since, including LightningLM V4's own Memory Stream; Ring Attention (2023-10-03, arXiv:2310.01889); RetNet (2023-07-17, arXiv:2307.08621); Mamba (2023-12-01, arXiv:2312.00752); and Differential Transformer (2024-10-07, arXiv:2410.05258), the one entry here that treats attention as wrong rather than expensive.

Three date corrections this exercise turned up, each of which the lecture or a common summary gets wrong:

The delta rule is 2021, not 2024. Schlag, Irie & Schmidhuber introduced it in February 2021 (arXiv:2102.11174). The 2024 paper parallelised it; Gated DeltaNet is a third, later paper (December 2024, arXiv:2412.06464). Three papers, three years, routinely collapsed into one.

Sparse attention is 2019, not DeepSeek's. Child, Gray, Radford & Sutskever at OpenAI, April 2019 (arXiv:1904.10509). DeepSeek's real contribution, six years later, is making it natively trainable — which is a better claim than inventing it.

Attention is 2014, not 2017. Bahdanau, Cho & Bengio, September 2014 (arXiv:1409.0473). 2017 removed the recurrence; it did not invent the mechanism.
